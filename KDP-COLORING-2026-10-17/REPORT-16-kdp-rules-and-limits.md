## 1. Short answer (12 lines)

1. **Method.** See above: search excerpts only; nothing verified against a live page.
2. **Agent question: not shown to be compatible.** Nothing found bans a disclosed agent by name and nothing approves one. The Conditions of Use "Agent Terms" let Amazon refuse any agent "at our sole discretion", so there is no safe harbor.
3. **No carve-out for supervision.** The Agent Terms apply when "you use, allow, enable, or cause the deployment of an Agent" and define an Agent as software acting "on behalf of, or at the instruction of, any person or entity" (definition wording seen in other Amazon texts). No sentence was found treating software run by the signed-in account holder differently from third parties.
4. **Written minimum if one is used:** "Agent/[name]" in the user agent of every HTTP/HTTPS request; do not hide that software is acting (no mimicking human typing or navigation speed); never complete or get around CAPTCHAs; answer "human or computer?" truthfully; stop when Amazon asks.
5. **KDP Terms 4.3** make the holder "responsible for all activities that occur under your account, whether or not you have authorized those activities" and say "You may not permit any third party to use the Program through your account". Nothing mentions software. A cautious reader treats a VA or contractor as a third party, treats the holder's own tool as untested, and puts all liability on the holder.
6. No KDP clause on bots, scraping or automation was found, and no Amazon sentence approving browser agents inside KDP. Full KDP Terms not read; whether the Conditions of Use also bind use of kdp.amazon.com is UNVERIFIED.
7. **Title limit: weekly, not daily.** 2 new titles per format (paperback, Kindle eBook, hardcover) per week to create (from 2026-09-21), plus 2 first-time submissions per format per week (from 2026-10-05; drafts count). Reset Sunday 12:00 AM UTC.
8. **So paperbacks: 2 a week, not 5-6 a day** (35-42 a week). The owner's plan is about 18-21 times over the cap. Older "3 per day" and "10 per week" figures on blogs are superseded. No current way to ask for more was found.
9. **Duplicate and low-quality risk: HIGH.** KDP lists as "disappointing" content "not significantly different from content in another book available in the Kindle Store" and content "excessively reused, recycled, or repeated within or across books" (a Kindle-focused guide); no numeric threshold. "Substantially similar" and "mass-produced" wording: NOT FOUND. KDP may reject, remove or block titles and may end an account over concerns about the account or related activity (paraphrase).
10. Coloring books are listed as "Not Generally Low-Content", so they are allowed. AI-generated images (cover or interior) must be disclosed on every new publish or republish, and heavy editing does not make them "AI-assisted".
11. **Scheduling and review.** Paperbacks can be scheduled ("schedule a release (print books only)", up to 90 days ahead); pre-order is eBook only. Normal review is up to 72 hours, but low-content reviews "may take 3-4 weeks".
12. **Bottom line.** Do not start live automation on this evidence. Get KDP's answer on agent use in writing, plan for 2 paperbacks a week, keep any backlog outside KDP, and have a person verify the key pages (or paste them) so these excerpts become verified quotes.

- R1. Nothing here is checked against a live page. Pages a person should re-read first: the Conditions of Use Agent Terms; KDP Terms sections 3 and 4; Create a Book; Content Guidelines (AI section); Low-Content Books; Timelines; Schedule a Release.
- R2. NOT FOUND: any KDP clause on bots or automation; any Amazon text approving browser agents in KDP; "substantially similar" or "mass-produced" wording; a way to raise the weekly cap; "black paper"; an "Unpublished" status; the browser-agent, computer-use or Nova Act names in the Conditions of Use.
- R3. Two documents may apply (Conditions of Use and KDP Terms). Which governs automated access to kdp.amazon.com is UNVERIFIED, so a cautious reader would meet both.

**Agent rules**
- R4. No safe harbor. Amazon may block any agent "at our sole discretion", and it has done so in practice (A6, H4). A disclosed agent can still be refused.
- R5. The user-agent token is required on "all HTTP/HTTPS requests". Open question: can our tooling add it to every request (pages, images, scripts, background calls) inside the owner's normal browser? If not, A2 cannot be met.
- R6. Do not add human-like random delays (A3 bars mimicking human speed). A person, not the software, handles sign-in, two-step verification and CAPTCHAs; the software should never hold the password or codes (cautious practice, tied to B3 and A3).
- R7. Confirm the account holder and the person or company running the software are the same legal person; otherwise B4 ("any third party") is a live issue.
- R8. Court coverage (secondary, UNVERIFIED): a preliminary injunction against Perplexity's Comet agent on 2026-03-09, reportedly lifted by an appeals court on 2026-08-04 on the ground that the user, not the vendor, was "accessing" Amazon. That concerns federal computer-fraud law, not Amazon's contract terms. Do not rely on it.

**Output, quality and money**
- R9. The cap is 2 per format per week, and it counts creation as well as first submission, drafts included. A "draft factory" inside KDP is capped too. Hold inventory outside KDP. eBook and hardcover caps are separate, but KDP says coloring books suit print (D4).
- R10. Duplicate risk has no published threshold. Near-identical interiors, covers or titles across a fast series match the language in D4. Consequences in the text: removal, Blocked status, account suspension or termination, no new account (B2), and forfeited unpaid royalties if terminated for breach of the Content Guidelines (B6, PARAPHRASE).
- R11. AI disclosure must be right every time (E1, E4). If the line art is AI-generated, the answer is yes. Intellectual-property checks are the publisher's job.
- R12. Review delay: low-content books "may take 3-4 weeks" (G4). Open question: will a coloring book be routed as low-content? KDP lists coloring books as "Not Generally Low-Content" (D2), but the review notice does not say.
- R13. Money: 60% royalty needs a US list price of $9.99 or more, else 50% (F10); short-book print cost is UNVERIFIED (F11). Check KDP's own calculator before pricing.
- R14. Conflicts to resolve by reading the live pages: spine text at 79 vs 80 pages (F8); schedule window 90 vs 5 to 90 days (G2); weekly cap across formats vs per format (C5); Conditions of Use date, 14 Aug 2026 vs other tracker dates (A1).
