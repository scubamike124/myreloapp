# Voice and compliance: what the system refuses, and why

Written 18 Oct 2026 (Pacific), revised 19 Oct after an independent code review (see [10-review-and-limits.md](10-review-and-limits.md)). Everything in this file describes code that exists and is tested (`src/lib/recruiting/voice.ts`, `compliance.ts`, `drafts.ts`, `actions.ts`, `state.ts`, `optout.ts`, `scrub.ts`). It is research turned into rules, not legal advice; the points that need a lawyer are listed at the end.

## The owner's rules, and where each one is enforced

| Rule from the brief | Enforced by | How |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing is sent until the messaging and compliance process is approved | `compliance.ts` (`process`), the page | Until the owner presses "Approve the process", every message is held and cannot be approved. The approval is one line in the owner's decision log with the date and scope, and can be withdrawn. |
| Amber does not send anything | The code | There is no sending code in the recruiting lane at all. The owner copies each approved message into their own mail and sends it, then presses "I sent it". |
| Natural, human-written wording; no robotic language, repetitive templates, generic AI wording or exaggerated claims | `voice.ts`, `drafts.ts` | See below. |
| No claim that Amber has clients, candidates or revenue without evidence | `voice.ts` (claims rules), `ledger.ts` | The wording check refuses claims about clients, placements and results. Revenue counts only with proof (a bank deposit, a payment record or a paid invoice), a reference of at least four characters, and the owner's confirmation. |
| Respect privacy, recruiting law and platform rules | `compliance.ts`, `regions.ts`, `contacts.ts`, `http.ts` | See "The gate" below. |
| Required human decisions | `actions.ts`, `state.ts` | Every approval, edit, rejection, "sent", reply, conversation, stage, opt-out, setting and money entry is an action by the owner, logged with its date. The worker writes prospects only. |
| Keep cost at $0 | `ledger.ts` | The budget defaults to $0. Any expense entered is counted and shown against it. Nothing in the lane calls a paid service. |

## The wording check (`voice.ts`)

Every message is read by this before the owner sees it. A message with an error is never offered as ready; a warning is shown but does not stop it. Each hit comes with its reason, so the owner can read why and disagree.

**It refuses (errors):**

- Stock openings and filler: "I hope this finds you well", "reaching out", "touching base", "circle back", "quick call", "I'd love to chat", "dear hiring manager", "please find attached", "feel free to".
- Words that sell instead of say: leverage, synergy, holistic, ecosystem, cutting-edge, world-class, game-changing, unlock, supercharge, seamless, robust, scalable, comprehensive, turnkey, bespoke, "tailored solutions", "in today's fast-paced world", "navigate the landscape", "our team of experts", "passionate about", "trusted partner".
- Text that gives away a machine reply: "as an AI", "here is your draft", "certainly!".
- Claims nobody can check: guarantees, "100%", "risk-free", "track record", "years of experience", "we have placed", "our clients", "trusted by", numbers of candidates or clients, rankings ("top", "best", "leading", "number one"), a license, certification or exclusivity Amber does not have, "AI-ranked", "bias-free", false urgency ("act now", "limited time", "urgent"), "no obligation".
- Closeness that was not earned: "I've been following your company", "big fan", "congratulations on", "as we discussed", "following up on our call". The sender has only read a careers page.
- All of the above **in the footer too**. The part under the `--` line is read for stock phrases, claims and false closeness, not only checked for its required lines (a client list or a promise written under the signature is an error). The sender's own name, business name and address are taken out first, so a business called "Best Staffing" is not read as a ranking.
- Emoji, unfilled placeholders (`{{name}}`, `[company]`, `TODO`, `undefined`), more than one exclamation mark, more than two long dashes, a subject that pretends to be a reply (`Re:`), shouting in a subject, more than two asks in one email, and a first email that does not name at least two things the reader can check (the company and the role, for example).

**It warns about (warnings):** length outside 45 to 130 words for an email; long dashes; words in capitals; the "not just X but Y" pattern; more than one list of three; every sentence about the same length; very long sentences; a message about "we" more than "you"; more than three sentences that start with "I"; bullets or stray spacing in a first email; no question; no plain greeting; a vague subject; one exclamation mark.

**What it cannot do.** It cannot make a sentence good. It refuses the sentences known to read as machine-made or as marketing, and it checks facts only in the sense that the message must name things the reader can verify. The owner reading each message is the real check, and the page is built around that.

**The batch check.** Ten messages that were each written for someone can still be one template with the names swapped. The composer picks among several hand-written versions of every sentence (openings, bridges, three offer sentences, terms, the ask, the sign-off, the subject), prefers the version used least in the batch, and tries up to 48 other combinations when a new message would share too much of its wording with one already made. Names, roles and places are taken out before comparing, and the comparison is on five-word runs. In the tests, across forty messages, the median pair shares under 12% of its wording and no pair reaches 40%. The page reports any pair at 30% or more instead of hiding it.

**Who read the page.** The notes say what the software did ("my software flagged the Healthcare Recruiter listing on your careers page") and never that a person read or saw it, because nobody has opened the page. They claim only what is true of every note: that the software flagged a listing. They do not say how the business was found (the lane does not record whether it came from a job board, a feed or the map), they say "your careers page" only for a role that was read on the company's own board with a vendor (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, SmartRecruiters, Workable, Recruitee, Personio), and a role read on a job feed or in a Hacker News thread is named for what it is ("on Remotive", "in a Hacker News hiring thread"). A role is mentioned only if a read saw it listed in the last two weeks, the rule the sample list uses; a firm with no such role and no known city gets no note. The firm is named once, in the greeting when the opening speaks to the reader ("you") and in the opening when it does not, because the wording check needs the firm named and a note that opens by naming the firm in the third person reads as a mail merge. Each note says once what the sender is not (a recruiter, a competitor), names one concrete thing, and asks one thing. Three layouts and several versions of every part keep ten notes in a week from sharing one skeleton; on twelve notes the median pair shares about 2% of its five-word runs and the worst pair 18%; on forty, the median pair about 4% and the worst 28%. They do not settle terms with a stranger: the price is mentioned in a minority of notes, softly, and never as a weekly list nobody asked for. The follow-up names the firm once, shows one real row and says plainly that it is the last note. The notes say "I go through what it finds", the offer says "Before a sample goes out I read it through myself and open a few of its links", and the workflow (04) tells the owner to do exactly that, because the promise is true only if the owner does it.

**Names and titles as a person writes them.** A business name is written the way someone would write to the business: no legal ending ("Brightpath Staffing Services, Inc." is "Brightpath Staffing Services"), no office it is listed under ("Dunmore & Pryce Staffing - Dallas North"), no "(USA)", no leading "The", and a name set in capitals ("PEOPLE FIRST STAFFING") is written as a name; "A. B. Chen & Co." is kept, because "& Co." is the brand. A job title is written as it would be said in a sentence: no "(Remote)", no " | Full Time" or " - Dallas" tail, "Sr." as "Senior". A name that ends in a period never leaves a doubled one. The cleaned name and title are the facts the wording check looks for, so what the note says and what is checked are the same words.

**Where the rules came from.** The owner's wording on 18 Oct 2026, plus the legal memo's section 6 ("never cross" list): pretending a person wrote or read something they did not, a persona that hides it is an AI, invented familiarity, invented clients, placements, results or testimonials, a license or exclusivity Amber lacks, "AI ranking" or "bias-free" without proof.

## The gate (`compliance.ts`)

A message is "ready" only if the gate says ok. A **hold** means something must be done first (a setting, a decision, a lawyer's reading). A **block** means it must not go out in this form at all. Anything unknown fails closed: a country that could not be read, a region with no recorded rule, a missing setting.

**Blocks**

- The business or address asked not to be contacted, or the owner set it aside. This is permanent.
- They already replied. A reply is handled by the owner by hand.
- The message is addressed to an individual job seeker. No cold message goes to a candidate, ever; candidates come in on their own.
- The wording check found an error.
- The service is not switched on in the settings.
- The region forbids cold email, or needs the recipient's prior consent (Germany, Spain, Italy, Canada, Australia, Japan, South Korea, South Africa).
- The address is not a role address on the company's own domain (`jobs@`, `careers@`, `hello@`, `info@`, `contact@`, `sales@`; never `first.last@`, `noreply@`, `privacy@`, `legal@`). The address on the draft differs from the one found on the company's page. The address (or the contact form) is not on the company's own website as it stands now: when the owner corrects a website, the mailbox found on the old one is blocked at once and dropped on the next pass, and the new site is read afresh.
- The footer lacks the sender's name, the business name, the postal address, the plain opt-out, the line saying how the address was found, the line saying it is a commercial message, or (unless the owner chose to omit it) the line saying an AI assistant is used. This holds for a note written into a company's contact form as much as for an email: a commercial message to a business is one either way.
- A first message was already sent to them, or a follow-up was already sent.

**Holds**

- The country could not be read, or no rule is recorded for it, or the region is not one the owner has cleared (default: United States only).
- The region's cold-email rules are restricted or unclear (Netherlands, Ireland, other EU/EEA countries, India, UAE, Brazil, Mexico).
- The service might count as employment-agency activity where licensing exists (shortlists, split fees, placement).
- The company's roles are in New York, New Jersey, Massachusetts or Illinois, where agency licensing is strict, until the owner has cleared the state after taking advice.
- No published address was found yet, or the page where it was printed is not recorded.
- A setting is missing (name, business name, postal address, reply address).
- A follow-up is not yet due. The clock counts whole days from the day typed when the first message was marked as sent, and the typed day has no time and no time zone, so the week is counted from the end of that day wherever the owner is: a message sent on the 12th can be followed up from noon UTC on the 20th (5 a.m. in Los Angeles), which is at least seven full days after it went out in any US time zone. A date typed when marking a message as sent cannot be earlier than the day before it was approved, so an invented old date cannot start the clock early.
- The process has not been approved.

**Notes** (shown, not stopping): a region's research is from public sources and a local lawyer should confirm before volume.

## How addresses are found and used (`contacts.ts`, `http.ts`)

1. The robots.txt of the company's site is read first. If it cannot be read, that counts as a refusal.
2. At most four pages are read: the home page and the usual contact, careers and about pages.
3. Only an address printed on the company's own site, on its own domain, and of a role kind is kept. For a staffing business the preference is sales, bd, hello, info, contact; for an employer it is jobs, careers, hr. A contact form is accepted as a channel but nothing is ever submitted by software.
4. A notice on the site saying it does not want unsolicited offers, or a sentence that probably says so, ends the search for that company (`src/lib/recruiting/notice.ts`). The reader has two parts. The first knows the usual shapes of such a sentence ("No agencies, please", "we do not accept unsolicited resumes from recruiters", "we do not work with third-party agencies", "this address is not for sales or agency enquiries", a line addressed to agencies telling them not to contact us) and calls it a notice. The second is wider: a sentence that has an unwelcome sender (an agency, a recruiter from outside, a vendor, a solicitor, a cold call), a refusal and a way of getting in touch in one sentence, said to or about outsiders, is passed on with its words. Both read **for whom a sentence is meant**: a sentence about candidates' resumes is nothing to a research vendor writing to a staffing firm (so it does not hold an agency), and is everything to a list of employers an agency will approach (so it holds an employer); a scam warning, a privacy line, a gift policy, a visitor rule, a description of how the firm itself works ("cold calling is not part of our sales process"), a description of the agencies it does work with ("we do not work with agencies that charge candidates") and a past-tense remark are not refusals; but a refusal of agencies that contact the company is one whatever money is mentioned ("we do not pay agencies that contact our staff"). The second part also reads a refusal named by its purpose ("do not call this office to sell us anything"), by the door it is shut at ("closed to ... agency reps", "will turn away ..."), by what is sold to the company ("we do not want a call about your outsourced services") and by a sender addressed in the first words ("Recruiters, ... please stop messaging them"); advice to the reader ("do not reply to unsolicited messages; forward them to security@...") is not a refusal by the company. The company is left out of the list and out of drafting. A notice found on one record of a website holds back every record of that site, as an opt-out does. A plain notice stays until the website is corrected. A **probable** notice shows its sentence on the business's card with a button: the owner reads it and, if it is not a request to be left alone, releases it. Every business that waits for that reading is listed first on the page, in a panel called "Waiting for you to read", whatever its rank, so none is lost below the cut of the list. The release names that exact sentence and that website, is kept in the record of the owner's actions, and lets the worker read the company again and take its address; a different sentence found later holds the company again. A false hold costs one prospect and one click; a missed notice costs a note to a company that said it wants none, so this reader is tuned the other way from the reply reader. The honest numbers are the first readings of text nobody had tuned it on: it held 65.5%, 72% and 70.9% of the notice sentences in three sets by three authors (about a quarter of them stated outright, the rest passed to the owner) and flagged about 3% of the ordinary sentences that use the same words (see 10). It still misses a notice worded in a way nobody here thought of, so the list says which companies' sites were actually read (a site that answered with an error, timed out or was closed to the reader by its robots.txt was not read, whatever was tried), and a company whose address was found is looked at again after six weeks.
5. Nothing is guessed, no email-finder or verifier service is used, and nothing is copied out of job posts, Hacker News comments or feed payloads. Poster names and emails in Hacker News posts are dropped when the post is read.
6. The reading identifies itself as `AmberRecruitingBot/1.0` with a contact address, at low request rates, at most 110 requests and about two minutes per run.

## Opt-outs

An opt-out is permanent and takes effect the moment the owner records it on the page. It can be by business, domain or address.

- **One business is often several records.** A staffing firm with an office in each city is one record per office on the map data, with one website and one mailbox. The records of one website are treated as one business: an opt-out given for one of them stops all of them, a first message sent through one is the first message to the business, and only one note is queued for it. The button sends the record's website and address along with its name, so a record found again later under another name stays set aside.
- **Spellings do not matter.** `www.`, a subdomain, a trailing dot, the scheme, upper case and plus-addressing (`jobs+spring@`) all match the same company; an opt-out from one mailbox is an opt-out of the whole company, because the person who answered speaks for the business.
- **The reply reader looks at the sender's own words, and is built to avoid the mistake that cannot be undone.** Filing a reply as an opt-out sets the whole business (and its website) aside, so a reply that WANTS the message must never be filed as one. Before anything is read, the quoted original ("On Monday … wrote:", lines starting with ">"), a forwarded copy, our own footer sentence (which itself says `reply "no thanks"`), the signature and a newsletter footer ("Unsubscribe from our job alerts") are cut away. What is left counts as an opt-out when it is a plain request to stop ("Take us off your list", "Stop contacting me", "Don't email me again", "I do not wish to receive further emails", "Unsubscribe", "No more emails", "We do not accept unsolicited emails") with nothing else attached to it, or a short bare "no thanks", "not interested" or "we'll pass" that asks for nothing and leaves no door open. A refusal written as a statement ("We do not accept unsolicited emails", "This is the last email", "Our managers are not to be contacted") counts only when the reply asks for nothing and leaves nothing open, and a reply that asks for the sample is never filed, however the rest is worded. A reply that asks a question, sends us to another person or address, limits only the phone or an attachment ("Don't call me, email is fine"), or says "not now, try us in Q1" is not filed: it stays a reply for the owner to read. The shared classifier (which reads "unsubscribe" anywhere in the text, quoted footers included) only sorts a reply into a category; it can no longer file an opt-out. A plain request in German, French, Spanish, Portuguese, Italian or Dutch is read too; a reply in any other language stays with the owner. Measured on 998 labelled replies in five sets: one request to stop missed and no reply wrongly filed, but those are development sets now, so that is not an estimate. The honest numbers are the first readings of text nobody had tuned the reader on: 4 mistakes in 104 (third review), 14 in 112 (fourth review: 1 wrongly filed, 13 of 53 requests missed), 14 in 120 (fifth set: 1 wrongly filed, 13 of 60 requests missed), 14 in 146 (fifth review: 1 wrongly filed, 13 of 63 requests missed, five of them in other languages) and 12 in 120 (sixth set: none wrongly filed, 12 of 60 requests missed). Expect about one request in five to be missed, and now and then a wrongly filed reply; the owner pastes every reply, so each is read by a person, and each mistake costs one click.
- **A reading can be wrong, so it can be undone.** A business set aside because a reply was read as an opt-out is listed first on the page, in "Waiting for you to read", and shows "That was not an opt-out: allow contact again" on its card, and the reply list says "filed as: do not contact again". Undoing it lifts the business and the website that reading had set aside. A "Never contact them" entry the owner made by hand cannot be undone from the page, and a reply read later never takes one over. The category recorded when the reply was logged is the one used when the log is folded; a later change to the reader does not move a business in or out of the list by itself.
- **The opt-out keeps the website it was given for.** The reply, like the button, records the business's website and address as they stood. A record's website that is corrected or re-read later does not move the opt-out: another record on the old website stays set aside.
- **Shared hosts are not one business.** A website on a site builder (`acme.wixsite.com`, `acme.vercel.app`, `acme.github.io`) is its own site under that name; the builder's front page and a profile page on LinkedIn, Facebook, a job board or an applicant-tracking system are nobody's own site and are never kept as a business's website. One customer of a site builder opting out does not set aside another.
- **A full record still takes an opt-out.** After 20,000 entries the record refuses ordinary actions, but a "never contact them", a logged reply or a withdrawal of the approval has a reserve of 500 entries of its own.
- **A suppressed business is not even read.** The worker skips it when it looks for an address, and the sample list leaves it out.
- The footer promises "I will not write again" and the system keeps that promise even if the owner forgets: a suppressed business can never appear in the queue, and a message that was already approved stops showing as approved and is shown as blocked.
- **Approvals lapse when the ground moves.** Changing any setting that decides what a message says or whether it may go out (name, business, address, reply address, AI line, services, regions, cleared states) ends every approval; changing it back does not bring them back unseen. Withdrawing the process does the same. Changing the budget or the sources leaves approvals alone.

## What nothing here does

- It does not score, rank, filter or reject a person. There are no people in the list.
- It does not send, post, submit a form, create an account or message anyone on any network.
- It does not build a profile of any person. An email address or a phone number in a listing or a post, and the name written next to it ("Senior Recruiter - Maria Gonzalez +1 (212) 555-0147" is kept as "Senior Recruiter"), is cut out when the text is read. A name written alone in a title cannot always be told from a role, so the guarantee is that no person is sought out, listed or profiled, not that a stray name can never pass through a public title.
- It does not claim a result. The ledger counts a dollar as revenue only with proof the owner has confirmed.

## What a lawyer should confirm before volume

Taken from the legal memo (section 9), reduced to what affects the first 90 days:

1. That a fixed-fee research list sold to staffing agencies is outside California's employment-agency statute (Civil Code 1812.500 and following; the employer-only exemption is at 1812.502(a)), and not agency activity where the agencies and employers are.
2. That the footer and the process meet CAN-SPAM and California B&P 17529.5 as the owner will use them (the postal address in particular).
3. Whether a research list that includes a company's published hiring address and the roles it advertises can bring Amber under California's data-broker rules. The list contains no named people, so the expectation is no, but this has not been confirmed.
4. Whether using public job-board feeds for a paid list is acceptable under each vendor's terms (most say nothing; see 03-lead-sources).
5. Before any employer-side or candidate-side work: the employer agreement, the candidate notice, retention, and the automated-hiring-tool rules in New York City, Illinois, Colorado, California and the EU (see 04-workflow).
