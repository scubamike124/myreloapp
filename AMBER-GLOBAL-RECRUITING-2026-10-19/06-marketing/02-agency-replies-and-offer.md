# Replies an agency owner is likely to need, and the one-page offer

The replies are short on purpose. The owner pastes or adapts them in their own mail. When an agency asks to stop, the owner records it on the page the same day.

## Replies

### They said yes to the sample

*Status: can be used once the owner has approved the process.* Reply to a positive answer. Attach the sample from the page's "Sample list" button.

Before it is used:
- the process is approved
- a sample exported from the page and checked by the owner

> Thanks for saying yes. The sample is attached as a spreadsheet.
>
> Each row has the company, the role, a link to the listing, the date it went up, and the hiring address the company publishes where I found one. Open two or three of the links before you read the rest. If the roles are still there and the dates match, the other rows were built the same way. If one looks wrong, tell me what you saw, because I want to find what caused it.

## The one-page offer

### What an agency gets: the one-page offer

*Status: can be used once the owner has approved the process.* Send after a yes, or paste into an email when someone asks for details. The prices in the text ($99 for four weekly lists, then $149 a month) are the suggestions in 05-revenue-plan, not a market price. Change them to your own before it goes.

Before it is used:
- the process is approved
- the owner has chosen the price (the figures in the text are suggestions)
- a sample the owner has checked

> Amber Global Recruiting reads public job listings and keeps a list of employers whose job ads were still up 45 days or more after they first appeared. This page says what you would get, what you would not, and how to stop.
>
> What you get: a spreadsheet once a week for the states and kinds of roles you choose. One row per company. Each row has the role, a link to the listing, the date it went up, and the hiring address the company publishes, where I found one. Recruiter jobs are left out, because a staffing firm's own openings are not what you are after. The sheet says where each row was read and which date it rests on.
>
> How the date works: if the job board gives a posting date, I use it. If it gives none, I count from the first day my software saw the listing and the sheet says so. A role may have been open longer than the sheet shows, and it may have been withdrawn since the page was read. Check the link before you call.
>
> What you do not get: names of people (I do not collect them on purpose), guessed email addresses, candidates, introductions, or any promise that a call turns into work. I am not placing anyone. I have no other clients yet, so there are no references to give you. The free sample is the way to judge whether the list is worth paying for.
>
> How it is made: the software reads job listings and company websites that anyone can open. On a company's own website it follows the site's rules for automated visitors, and it leaves the company off the list if a page there says it does not want approaches from agencies. It can miss a notice worded in a way it does not know, so look at a company's site before you write to it. Before a sample goes out I read it through myself and open a few of its links.
>
> What it costs: the first sample is free. A four-week trial of the weekly list is $99. After that it is $149 a month, agreed in writing before anything is billed, and you can stop at the end of any month without giving a reason.
>
> If you ever want to be left alone, one line from you is enough. Your address goes on a list of people I never write to, and I check that list before every message.

*415 words. Wording check: 0 errors, 0 warnings.*


## Setting the price

The research found no published price for lists like this, so the figures below are estimates built from freelance research rates ($25 to $60 an hour in the US) and the hours a weekly list takes (one to three). They are suggestions for the owner to change, not a market price.

| Offer | Suggested | Why |
|---|---|---|
| First sample | Free | The only way an unknown sender gets a first look |
| Pilot: four weekly lists | $99 | Small enough for a one-person agency to say yes without a meeting |
| Monthly, after the pilot | $149 (range $99 to $199) | About one to two hours of a freelancer's time each week; a fraction of one placement fee |
| Anything with a fee that depends on a hire | Not offered | See 02-voice-and-compliance and 09-viability |
