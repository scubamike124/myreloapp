# Employer note: held for stage two

**Do not use.** The list product sells to staffing agencies and never touches an applicant's details. This note would offer something different: reading a small employer's applications and writing down, for each thing the employer says matters, what the applicant wrote about it. That needs an agreement with the employer about who is responsible for the applicants' details, a notice to applicants, and a check against the rules on automated hiring tools (New York City, Illinois, Colorado, California and the EU). None of that is done. The composer and the compliance gate also cover agencies only; they would have to be extended for employers before anything is composed or approved on the page.

It is here so the owner can see what the wording would be, and so a lawyer can read what is being proposed.

### First note to a small employer with a long-open role (example: a made-up company)

*Status: HELD: do not use.* Not in use. This is the wording the stage-two offer would start from. Employers with no agency between them and the candidates are the larger market, and also the one with the most rules (see 04-workflow and 02-voice-and-compliance).

Before it is used:
- a lawyer has read how applicants' details are handled and the agreement between the employer and Amber

> Hello Northwind team,

## What would have to happen first

1. The owner decides to offer it at all and sets a flat fee per role. The only comparison found is freelance screening work at $25 to $60 an hour; at that rate reading forty applications is roughly $175 to $420, so a flat fee of $150 to $300 per role is a starting estimate, not a market price.
2. A lawyer reads the draft agreement and the applicant notice, and says whether a sheet that only quotes the applicant (and scores and ranks nobody) stays outside the automated-hiring-tool rules in the places where the employer hires.
3. The page's gate and composer are extended for employers, and the owner approves that wording separately.
4. A written agreement with the employer exists before any applicant's details reach Amber.
