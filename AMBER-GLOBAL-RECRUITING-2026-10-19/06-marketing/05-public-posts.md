### A checklist for cold email in the US

*Status: can be used once the owner has approved the process.* A useful post for agency owners and small employers. It says plainly that it is not legal advice.

Before it is used:
- the process is approved

> Not legal advice, just the checklist I am going to follow before I send anything. If you send cold email to companies in the US, CAN-SPAM covers business addresses as well as personal ones. That means a real postal address in the message, an opt-out that works, a subject line that says what the message is, and no pretending to be someone you are not. Honor the opt-out quickly.

### What a useful applicant summary looks like

*Status: can be used once the owner has approved the process.* For small employers. It states the principle a candidate summary would follow if Amber ever offers one; nothing like that exists yet.

Before it is used:
- the process is approved

> A useful summary of an applicant is a few lines per requirement: what they wrote about it, in their words, and whether they wrote anything at all. It is not a score and it is not a rank.
>
> If the summary cannot point to a sentence the applicant wrote, it should say "not stated". Whoever decides should be able to check every line against the resume in under a minute.

## Weekly status post (a format, not a text)

Written on the day from the owner's page, never in advance: roles seen, roles open 45 days or more, messages sent, replies, requests to stop, money in and out with proof. If a number is zero, the post says zero. A post with a made-up or rounded-up number is the quickest way to lose the trust this business depends on.
