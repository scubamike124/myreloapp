Research date: 19 October 2026. Public web only. No outreach, sign-ups, purchases or posting.

**Key.** FACT [n] = stated on source n (URL and date in the numbered list at the end). ESTIMATE = my reasoning, shown. UNVERIFIED = could not confirm. "V" after a source number = the source sells something (vendor, market-research seller, trade network); treat as marketing.

**Bottom line.** The employer-paid slice Amber can reach without payroll (direct hire) is about $11B a year in the US (SIA), fragmented, cyclical, and flat to down in 2025. ESTIMATE: a new firm with no track record could probably earn small first revenue from fixed-price sourcing and research for small agencies, but only if the owner allows outreach. Under the current "no outreach, no sign-ups, no posting" rule, revenue is impossible.

## 1. Market size and shape

**Global**
- FACT: SIA estimated the 2024 global staffing market at $619B [1] and 2025 at about $639B (EUR 566B), up 2%, with about +3% expected in 2026; EMEA is about 42%. The 100 largest firms hold about 40% [2]. (The excerpt did not say which of the two SIA news pages carried each figure.) ESTIMATE: $639B at +2% implies a restated 2024 base near $626B, not $619B, so SIA revises its history; compare figures only within one edition.

**US**
- FACT: SIA, September 2026: US staffing +2.4% in 2026 to $183.1B, then +2.2% in 2027 to $187.0B [3]. Its March 2026 edition had +1% to $180.2B [4]. SIA also headlines a cumulative +10% for 2025 to 2030 [6], about 2% a year.
- ESTIMATE: the 2025 base is about $179B ($183.1B / 1.024). SIA's March 2025 edition had forecast $188.7B for 2025 [5], so SIA restated its baseline by roughly 5%. Do not mix editions.
- FACT: ASA: temp and contract sales were $142.4B in 2023, down 10.5% from a record $159.1B in 2022; staffing gave jobs to about 11M people in 2024 (about 2.2M in an average week) [7]. Q2 2026: total staffing revenue +3.3% year on year, employment +0.6% [8].
- FACT: ASA counts about 27,000 staffing and recruiting companies in about 54,000 offices (2021 data) [7]. IBISWorld counts 5,479 US executive-search recruiter businesses [13]. ESTIMATE: with the top 24 direct-hire firms holding only 19% of that segment (below), the rest is a long tail of small firms.

**Segments (what Amber could sell without payroll)**
- Direct hire / contingency placement. FACT: SIA puts US direct hire at about $11B in 2025; the 24 firms with at least $25M each made $2.1B, about 19% of the market [9]. 2024 was $11.2B [10]. Global direct hire was $50.4B in 2021, 8.4% of global staffing [11]. ESTIMATE: that is about 6% of US staffing ($11B / $179B), with about $8.9B earned outside the top 24. A 2025 global figure is UNVERIFIED (SIA's report is members-only); 6% to 8.4% of $639B would be about $38B to $54B.
- Executive / retained search. FACT: 2025 global estimates run from $18B to $58B across three market-research sellers [12 V], a threefold spread, so treat the global size as unknown. IBISWorld: US $10.2B in 2026, +2.9% in 2026 but -3.3% a year over 2021 to 2026 (published May 2026) [13]. Its scope may overlap SIA's direct-hire figure (UNVERIFIED).
- RPO. FACT: about $10.2B to $11B globally in 2025 per two market-research sellers, who forecast 10% to 13% yearly growth [19 V]. ESTIMATE: that conflicts with SIA's low-single-digit outlook, so treat it as marketing. SIA's RPO report is members-only.
- Sourcing support and research: no size found (UNVERIFIED). It is sold inside agencies and RPOs, not tracked as a segment.

**Reads from public companies**
- FACT: Robert Half permanent placement revenue was $440M in 2025, down 9.8%; placements fell 13.6% while the average fee per placement rose 3.8%; its contract (temp) revenue was $2.99B, down 11.0% [14]. Q2 2026: $118M, up 2.9%, 13.6% of its Talent Solutions revenue [15]. Korn Ferry FY26 (to 30 April 2026): fee revenue $2.9B, +7%; Professional Search and Interim +14%, Executive Search +7%, RPO +5% [16]. Hays FY26 (to 30 June 2026): net fees -8% like for like to GBP 905.5M, perm weaker than temp (-12% vs -5% per a secondary report; check Hays' release) [17]. PageGroup H1 2026 gross profit GBP 385.2M, -1.1% reported [18].
- ESTIMATE: at Robert Half (one company), fee levels held in 2025 while volume fell.

**Shape in plain words (ESTIMATE).** Perm and search are a small, cyclical, fragmented slice (about 6% of US staffing). It shrank in 2025 and is recovering slowly in 2026. Demand signals are positive but vendor-sourced: 66% of 2,000+ US hiring managers told Robert Half they plan to raise perm hiring in H2 2026, versus 57% a year earlier [22 V]; SIA's staffing confidence index hit a post-pandemic high in June 2026 [21]. Temp staffing is most of the market but needs payroll float, so it is out of reach at $0. The in-house substitute is large: BLS counts 939,700 HR specialists (the occupation that includes recruiters), median pay $75,940, +6% to 2035 [20].

## 2. Fee structures

- **Contingency placement.** FACT: SIA's survey of over 300 firms (2021 data, published April 2022): median fee 20% of first-year salary; most common 20% (42% of firms); midrange 15-20% for commercial firms, 18-22% for professional firms, 20-25% for direct-hire specialists [23]. By role level there is no neutral breakdown. ESTIMATE: entry and clerical 15-20%, mid-level professional 18-22%, senior or specialist 20-25%; vendors claim 25-35% for tech and executive roles (UNVERIFIED) [28 V]. Example: 20% of $90,000 = $18,000. Public bids: Sierra HR Partners charged 15% of first-year salary, half as retainer, with a 90-day guarantee (Fresno LAFCo staff report, January 2022) [24]; in Osage County, Kansas (February 2026) one bidder offered about 20% with a free replacement within 90 days, another a flat $22,500 with a 50%-of-fee replacement [25].
- **Retained search.** FACT: one-third of first-year total cash compensation in three instalments, the first non-refundable [27]. A University of Wisconsin document shows Korn Ferry terms of a one-third retainer and a 12-month guarantee with a free replacement search (expenses still billed), with minimum fees of $60,000 (one firm) and $80,000 (Korn Ferry) [26]. One vendor source says $75,000-$100,000 at top-tier firms (UNVERIFIED). Vendors quote 25-35% [28 V].
- **RPO.** Vendor figures only [29 V]: monthly management fee, per-hire fee, or hybrid; per-hire 5-10% of salary; $4,000 to $15,000 a month per dedicated recruiter. Needs scale and references.
- **Flat fee per hire.** Vendor guides [30 V]: entry $1,000-$3,000, mid-level $3,000-$7,500, senior $7,500-$15,000. Per-shortlist pricing: none found. ESTIMATE: for a shortlist (5 vetted, contactable candidates) a newcomer's price ceiling is $150-$900, because freelance labour at $25-$60/hour for an assumed 6-15 hours sets the comparison.
- **Hourly sourcing.** Upwork's guides: $30-$60/hour for talent acquisition specialists, $25-$70 in another guide, US recruiters $50-$150 [31 V]. Actual Upwork sourcing postings: $8-$15, $10-$12 and $20-$25 an hour [32]. Philippine recruiting assistants: about $6.79/hour (about $1,087/month) on one platform; managed seats from $10/hour or $1,600-$2,400/month; all-in seats $1,800-$2,000 [33 V]. Day rates: none found. **What small agencies actually pay for overflow sourcing: UNVERIFIED, no survey.** ESTIMATE: $8-$25/hour offshore, $25-$60/hour onshore freelance, $1,600-$2,400/month for a managed offshore seat. A founder-run AI service competes with these.
- **Split fees.** 50/50 is the usual reference; networks and blogs say splits range to 70/30 in favour of the side contributing more, agreed deal by deal [35 V]. Marketplaces: BountyJobs paid recruiters 75% and kept 25% (2008 profile) [36]; Paraform says recruiters keep "the majority", is closed (approval needed), pays recruiters in thirds monthly over 90 days, and gives employers a 90-day money-back guarantee [37 V].
- **Employee-referral bounties** (paid by employers to their own staff). FACT: a 2024 survey of 52 mostly Midwest employers averaged $1,936 [38]; vendor pages say $1,000-$5,000, tech about $5,000, retail about $500 [38 V]. A SHRM $2,500 median is UNVERIFIED. These are small next to a $18,000 agency fee and go to employees, not outsiders.
- **Guarantees.** FACT: in a recruiter poll, 90 days was most common among firms offering one (44.9%), then 30 days (20.3%) and 60 days (20.0%); 1% offered none [39 V]. Retained search runs to 12 months. The remedy is usually a replacement search or partial refund.
- ESTIMATE (cash timing): perm fees are invoiced at the start date, so cash arrives weeks to months after sourcing, with clawback risk in the guarantee window.

## 3. First-dollar paths (rankings are ESTIMATE)

Any path needs at least one outside contact. Under the current rules no path can earn anything. The ranking assumes the owner lifts the rules for named steps. Unless a number carries a source tag, everything in this section is ESTIMATE.

| Rank | Path | Speed to first $ | Cash capital | Regulatory exposure | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sourcing/shortlist micro-projects for small agencies and solo recruiters | 4-12 weeks | $0 | Low-moderate | Best first move |
| 2 | Business-development research for the same agencies | 2-8 weeks | $0 | Low (company data) to moderate-high (named people) | Pair with 1 |
| 3 | Split-fee participation | 3-9 months | $0 | Moderate | Add-on only |
| 4 | Flat-fee or hybrid shortlists for small employers | 8-20 weeks | $0 | Moderate-high | Stage two |
| 5 | Referral-style introductions; job-posting distribution | n/a | n/a | n/a | Unrealistic |

**1. Agency sourcing micro-projects.** Sale: $150-$900 per shortlist, or $25-$60/hour equivalent. Buyers know what a shortlist is and can test one cheaply. Cash capital is $0, but tool access is the real limit: a LinkedIn Recruiter Corporate seat is quoted at $900-$1,080 a month by vendor pages [47 V], so Amber is limited to free public sources. Regulatory: California's employment-agency act does not apply to a person charging fees only to employers (news-site summary of Civil Code 1812.502; verify the statute) [40]. The risks are candidate personal data and any automated scoring (below). Caveat: demand is unproven. Small recruiters report their pain as winning clients, not finding candidates (section 4), so some will not need sourcing help.

**2. Agency BD research.** Sale: ESTIMATE $50-$250 per pilot list (1-3 hours of work at the $25-$60/hour anchor, rounded up); no price evidence found. Same buyers as path 1, so one outreach push serves both. California's Delete Act requires "data brokers" (businesses that knowingly collect and sell personal information of people they have no direct relationship with) to register yearly, and since 1 August 2026 to process deletion requests through the DROP platform at least every 45 days [41]. Whether bespoke research for one client counts as a "sale" is UNVERIFIED; get advice before delivering lists of named individuals. Crowded with data tools (not researched).

**3. Split-fee participation.** At 20% on $90,000, a 50/50 split pays about $9,000. Cash comes only after submission, interview, offer, start, invoice and payment, then a guarantee window. Odds are low: in a trade vendor's 2026 survey the biggest bucket of recruiters made 1-5 placements and 48.2% reported revenue under $200K [53 V]. Reasoning: established recruiters tend to share their hardest job orders, not their best. Use it as an add-on once an agency trusts Amber.

**4. Small-employer shortlists.** Demand signal: NFIB's September 2025 survey found 32% of owners with openings they could not fill, and 50% of all owners (88% of those hiring) reporting few or no qualified applicants [46]. The same scarcity makes shortlists hard to deliver. Many small buyers, a trust gap, and free alternatives (own network, job boards) lengthen the cycle. Regulatory: California's rules effective 1 October 2025 define an employer's "agent" to include anyone doing recruiting or screening for it, including with automated tools [42]; in Mobley v. Workday (still pending) the court let an "agent" theory against an AI vendor proceed, but rejected an "employment agency" theory because Workday did not recruit candidates [43]. ESTIMATE: a firm that does recruit candidates may sit on the other side of that line (UNVERIFIED as law; get advice). Keep a human making every evaluative call.

**Unrealistic, plainly**
- Referral-style introductions: bounties belong to employees; an outsider has no standing and no terms of business. Employer treatment of unsolicited resumes was not researched.
- Job-posting distribution: a commodity needing ad spend or posting accounts, both barred; no edge (reasoning only, not researched).
- RPO: needs scale, references and enterprise procurement.
- Retained search: buyers pay one-third retainers to credentialed firms, with minimum fees of $60,000-$80,000 in one university contract [26]; no track record means no mandate.
- Temp staffing: payroll float and insurance.

AI-hiring rules, for context: Colorado's AI Act was delayed to 30 June 2026 [44] (current status UNVERIFIED). One law firm reports the EU's high-risk obligations for recruitment AI moved from 2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027; adoption is reported inconsistently [45]. A parallel file in this folder (02-legal-compliance.md, written by another agent) covers licensing in more depth, including regimes for placement fees outside California; I have not verified it.

## 4. Competitors and substitutes

**AI tools (saturated, well funded)**
- FACT: LinkedIn announced its Hiring Assistant agent on 29 October 2024 [48]. A competitor's blog says it reached general availability in English in late September 2025 with no public price, sold as an add-on to LinkedIn Recruiter (UNVERIFIED) [48 V].
- FACT: Juicebox raised $80M at an $850M valuation on 10 March 2026 and reports 5,000 customers [49]. Two roundups count $656M to $735M raised by AI-recruiting startups in the year to mid-2026 (methods differ) [50 V].
- FACT: Bullhorn's 2026 report (about 2,300 respondents, 25 February 2026): top-performing firms are four times more likely to use AI; only 10% have agentic AI across the whole workflow [51 V]. SIA lists "Direct Sourcing Further Undermines Intermediary Sourcing" as a 2026 trend (headline only) [52].

**Marketplaces and offshore**
- Paraform: contingency, closed marketplace, reports 1,000+ customers and $65M raised (roundups describe the round differently) [37 V]. BountyJobs: acquired by Recruiter.com in November 2024 per Dealroom [36]. Upwork is open and hourly [31]. Offshore firms such as QX Global sell dedicated recruiters to US staffing firms but publish no price [34 V].

**Saturated:** AI sourcing and outreach, ATS-embedded AI, offshore labour at $8-$25/hour, LinkedIn.

**Under-served (ESTIMATE; evidence is indirect)**
- Client-acquisition help for small agencies (their top stated priority).
- Narrow-niche or cross-border sourcing that a small agency cannot staff.
- Documented, human-reviewed shortlists as AI-hiring rules tighten (but this raises Amber's own exposure).
- Authenticity checks: Gartner predicts one in four candidate profiles worldwide could be fake by 2028; 6% of 3,000 surveyed candidates admitted interview fraud [54].
- Small employers who cannot find applicants [46].

**What small agencies complain about.** FACT, trade-vendor survey, self-selected, sample size not shown [53 V]: new clients are the number-one priority for 60.7%; the economy is the biggest threat for 62.0%; the biggest placement bucket is 1-5; 48.2% report revenue under $200K; saturation brings fee compression; recruiters handle 93% more applications than in 2021; generalists struggle. The same vendor runs an article titled "Why Recruiters Are Struggling More With Clients Than Candidates" (title only). Tool cost: no floating LinkedIn Recruiter licences, per vendor pages [47 V]. A competitor's blog says hiring managers source in-house below VP level [28 V].

## 5. Cold outreach reality

- FACT (all-industry B2B, vendors): Instantly 2026 platform data: average reply rate 3.43%, top quartile 5.5%, "elite" above 10%; 58% of replies come from the first email [55 V]. Belkins (agency): 5.8% across 16.5M emails; campaigns under 50 recipients 5.8% vs 2.1% at 500+ [56 V].
- FACT (recruiting-specific, weak): BounceZero lists recruiting/HR at 3-6% average, 7-10% "good" [57 V]. Pin reports 4.96% over 4M+ recruiting messages, almost certainly candidate outreach rather than client sales (UNVERIFIED) [57 V]. One outbound agency claims 8-12% for staffing (UNVERIFIED marketing). A roundup warns published benchmarks differ up to 7.6x by method and cites a strict measure of 0.45% (UNVERIFIED).
- **No source found** for reply-to-meeting-to-paying-client conversion in staffing.
- ESTIMATE funnel: replies 2-5% of sends, 40-60% positive, 10-25% of conversations become a paid pilot. That is one pilot per 130 to 1,250 well-targeted contacts, central about 400. At 50 founder-reviewed contacts a week, that is roughly 3 to 25 weeks, central about 8.
- Sales cycle: no recruiting-specific benchmark. Generic vendor blogs: small deals 2-6 weeks; new-business deals average 91 days (Ebsta/Pavilion 2025, relayed second-hand) [58 V]. An ERE staffing article says weeks or months of trust-building precede a first order (undated) [58]. ESTIMATE for a micro-pilot: 1-4 weeks after a positive reply, paid on delivery.
- Objections (practitioner blogs [59 V]): "we already have a preferred supplier list or VMS"; "we already use an agency"; "your fees are too high"; "we recruit internally". ESTIMATE for Amber: no track record or references; AI-sourced candidates and privacy; confidentiality and candidate ownership; "why not a $10/hour offshore sourcer?".
- Email-law rules (CAN-SPAM, EU, UK, Canada) were not researched. Check before any send. ESTIMATE: at $0, mail goes from a free mailbox, which risks deliverability, so benchmark reply rates (from tuned platforms) are probably optimistic for Amber.

## 6. Verdict

**Viable only as a small, slow side business at first. Not viable as a fast full-income business at $0. Not viable at all under the current no-outreach rule.**

- **Time to first paying client (ESTIMATE, judgment not data; everything in this section is ESTIMATE unless tagged):** central about 10 weeks from first message to a first paid micro-pilot of $150-$900; range 3 weeks to never. The section 5 funnel alone would imply higher odds; I cut them for a free mailbox, no track record and unproven demand. About 40-50% chance of at least one paid pilot within 90 days of steady outreach (about 50 targeted messages a week), 60-75% within six months. First placement-fee cash: 4-9 months, possibly none in year one. About 10-20% chance of $3,000 a month recurring within 12 months. Reaching $3,000 a month needs about 3-10 shortlists a month at $300-$900, or one 50/50 split a quarter.
- **Main risks**
  1. Price ceiling set by offshore and AI labour ($8-$60/hour) [31-33].
  2. Demand mismatch: stated pain is clients, not candidates [53].
  3. Weak perm market: Robert Half perm -9.8% in 2025, Hays perm about -12% in FY26 [14, 17].
  4. No track record or references.
  5. Sourcing access at $0 limits quality; platform terms of service UNVERIFIED.
  6. AI-hiring liability: California agent rules, Workday case, EU timing [42, 43, 45].
  7. Personal-data exposure (Delete Act; GDPR not researched) [41].
  8. Cash timing and guarantee clawbacks.
- **What would raise the verdict:** an agency owner agreeing to a design-partner pilot; permission to spend a few dollars a month on a domain and mailbox (price UNVERIFIED); a niche with real scarcity and lawful access; two or three written testimonials; continued perm recovery.
- **What would lower it:** under 5 replies from about 300 targeted contacts; LinkedIn bundling AI sourcing cheaply; rules treating sourcing vendors as agents; buyers who will pay only $10/hour.
- **Tripwires:** stop or pivot if, after about 300 targeted contacts over about 6 weeks, there are fewer than 3 positive conversations, or if none of the first 3 pilots reorders.

**Leads I could not read:** SIA headline "Sales difficulty hits record while bill rate optimism trends downward" (staffingindustry.com/editorial/cws-30-contingent-workforce-strategies/sales-difficulty-hits-record-while-bill-rate); SIA North America Staffing Company Survey 2024 full report; SIA Largest Direct Hire Staffing Firms Globally 2026; NPAworldwide split rules (members' materials).
