## 2. Recruiter and employment-agency licensing

**California (the home base).** The statute is Civil Code Title 2.91, not the Labor Code or Business and Professions Code. An "employment agency" is a person who, for a fee paid by a job seeker, procures jobs or registers job seekers (https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-3/part-4/title-2-91/chapter-1/section-1812-501/, 2025 text). It is a bond and contract regime, not a licence: a $3,000 surety bond filed with the Secretary of State, 24-month minimum term, $30 filing fee (https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-3/part-4/title-2-91/chapter-2/section-1812-503/, 2025; https://bpd.cdn.sos.ca.gov/sf/forms/sf-sb-449.pdf, 2015 form). Crucially, s.1812.502(a) says the title "does not apply" to a person providing those services who "charges fees exclusively to employers" (https://law.justia.com/codes/california/2018/code-civ/division-3/part-4/title-2.91/chapter-1/section-1812.502/, 2018 text; current text unverified). Labor Commissioner licensing is for talent agencies (https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/talent/talent_laws_relating_to_talent_agencies.pdf, 2025). **[LAWYER]** to confirm the employer-only exemption still reads this way.

**Other US states (federal licensing: none found, unverified).**
- New York: fee-charging agencies need a licence; DOL describes an agency as any entity charging a fee to place a candidate with a third-party employer (https://dol.ny.gov/employment-agencies, n.d.). GBL s.185: no fee may be collected by an unlicensed agency (https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/185, n.d.).
- Massachusetts: staffing agencies must be licensed as employment agencies or registered as placement agencies (https://www.mass.gov/info-details/information-for-employment-agencies, n.d.).
- New Jersey: employment agencies licensed by Consumer Affairs; temp help firms need certification, $250, bond, rules adopted 2024-09-16 (https://www.njconsumeraffairs.gov/epservices, n.d.; https://business.nj.gov/recent/new-requirements-for-temporary-help-service-firms-and-their-clients, n.d.).
- Illinois: Private Employment Agency Act licence; Day and Temporary Labor Services Act registration, equal pay after 720 hours (https://www.ilga.gov/Legislation/ILCS/Articles?ActID=2417&ChapterID=68, n.d.; https://labor.illinois.gov/content/dam/soi/en/web/idol/laws-rules/fls/dtlsa/DTLSA_06052025_final%20.pdf, 2025-06-05).
- Texas and Pennsylvania: sources conflict (unverified). Other states not researched. Whether an out-of-state firm is caught when the job or candidate is in these states: **[LAWYER]**.

**Outside the US.**
- UK: no general licence found; gangmaster licensing (now Fair Work Agency) covers agriculture, shellfish, food processing and packaging (https://www.gov.uk/employment-agencies-and-businesses/licences-for-employment-agencies, n.d.; https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-fair-work-agency-licensing-standards-for-gangmasters, n.d.). Conduct Regulations 2003 apply (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/3319/pdfs/uksi_20033319_en.pdf, 2003).
- Germany: temporary agency work needs a Federal Employment Agency permit (https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/unternehmen/personalfragen/pflichten-arbeitgeber/arbeitnehmerueberlassung/erlaubnis-arbeitnehmerueberlassung, n.d.). Pure placement: no permit believed, unverified.
- France: placement may be for profit; no licence found for pure placement; temp-work agencies have a separate regime, unverified (https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000022517620, n.d.).
- Spain: agencies file a "declaración responsable" with the regional employment service (Ley 3/2023 art.43), valid nationally (https://www.boe.es/buscar/pdf/2023/BOE-A-2023-5365-consolidado.pdf, 2023, consolidated; summary via https://www.sepe.es/HomeSepe/en/encontrar-trabajo/agencias-colocacion.html, n.d.).
- Italy: authorisation and entry in the ANPAL register for intermediation and for "ricerca e selezione del personale" (search and selection); requires a company form, an EU legal seat and qualified staff; capital figures conflict (https://www.lavoro.gov.it/strumenti-e-servizi/pagine/albo-nazionale-delle-agenzie-il-lavoro, n.d.; https://www.lavoro.gov.it/archivio-doc-pregressi/Strumenti_normativa/2003/20030910_DLGS_276.pdf, 2003 text).
- Canada: Ontario has required licences for temporary help agencies and recruiters since 2024-07-01, and employers may not knowingly use an unlicensed recruiter (https://www.ontario.ca/page/licensing-temporary-help-agencies-and-recruiters, n.d.; https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/r24478, 2024). Quebec: CNESST permit for personnel placement agencies and temporary-foreign-worker recruiters (https://www.cnesst.gouv.qc.ca/fr/demarches-formulaires/agences-placement-personnel-recrutement/permis-agences, 2026). BC: licensing page says no licence if recruiting for one employer only (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/employment-standards-advice/employment-standards/licensing/licensing-employment-agencies, n.d.).
- Australia: labour-hire licensing in Queensland (recruitment and permanent placement excluded), South Australia (all industries from the 2026-07-29 deadline stated on the page; confirm), ACT; Victoria and WA not verified (https://www.labourhire.qld.gov.au/licensing, 2026; https://ablis.business.gov.au/service/sa/labour-hire-licence/44188, 2026).
- Singapore: an Employment Agency licence is needed to place job seekers with employers; wholly web-based job portals and bulletin boards that do no matching are exempt (https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-agencies/eligibility-and-requirements/who-needs-to-get-a-licence, n.d.).
- India, UAE: not researched. Treat as licensed activities until local advice says otherwise (unverified).
- ILO C181 requires states to run a licensing or certification system (art.3(2)) and bars worker fees (art.7) (https://www.ilo.org/resource/other/c181-private-employment-agencies-convention-1997, 1997). About 40 ratifications, including France, Italy, Spain, the Netherlands and Japan (https://normlex.ilo.org/dyn/nrmlx_en/f?p=NORMLEXPUB%3A11300%3A0%3A%3ANO%3A%3AP11300_INSTRUMENT_ID%3A312326, 2026).

## 3. Charging job seekers

- ILO C181 art.7: no fees or costs to workers, directly or indirectly; exceptions only for defined worker groups after consultation (https://www.ilo.org/resource/other/c181-private-employment-agencies-convention-1997, 1997).
- UK: work-seekers cannot be charged for finding work; entertainment and modelling are the exceptions (Schedule 3). Separate optional services (CV help, training) are allowed with written notice first and no detriment for withdrawing (regulation numbers 13 and 26 as described in the gov.uk excerpt; check numbering) (https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/conduct-regulations-2003-guidance-for-employment-agencies-and-employment-businesses/overview-of-the-conduct-regulations-2003, n.d.).
- France: no direct or indirect payment from job seekers for placement; penalty six months' prison and €3,750 (L5321-3, L5324-1) (https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000022520917, n.d.).
- Spain: free intermediation for workers (Ley 3/2023). British Columbia: no payment for finding work or for employer information; payments are treated as wages owed (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/employment-standards-advice/employment-standards/forms-resources/igm/esa-part-2-section-10, n.d.). Massachusetts: no charging workers for registering or procuring a job (https://www.mass.gov/info-details/information-for-employment-agencies, n.d.). Singapore: caps for foreign workers (1 month's salary per year of service, max 2 months) (https://www.mom.gov.sg/-/media/mom/documents/employment-agencies/guide-on-new-ea-regulatory-framework.pdf, n.d.).
- **California:** an "employment counseling service" (career counselling, vocational guidance, aptitude testing, career planning, résumé development, for a fee) has its own rules: $10,000 bond, written contract, three-business-day cancellation, unless the provider charges strictly by the hour with no obligation beyond the hourly fee (https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-civ/division-3/part-4/title-2-91/chapter-3/section-1812-510/, 2024-25; older text had a résumé-only exemption capped at $300, current status unverified). A "job listing service" that charges job seekers is also covered.

**Meaning for Amber:** keep the candidate side free in the first 90 days. If paid coaching or résumé review is added later, run it as a separate, hourly-only service never tied to introductions or Amber's employer network, and do not sell it into the UK, France, Spain or other fee-ban countries without local advice. **[LAWYER]**

**In plain terms.** What keeps a workflow lighter (my reading; **[LAWYER]** to confirm): AI that only drafts, summarises, formats, de-duplicates or finds public company facts; a named human who reads the source material and makes every decision to contact, shortlist or reject; no numeric score, rank or auto-reject; criteria set by the client and shown as visible evidence; no video, voice, emotion or personality inference; no protected-trait proxies such as zip code; decision logs kept at least four years (the California rule). What does not help: a human glancing at an AI ranking. NYC covers tools that "substantially assist" decisions, California covers systems that facilitate human decisions, and EU high-risk status depends on the tool's purpose, not on whether a person signs off (unverified detail). Human review mainly helps with GDPR Art.22, which concerns solely automated decisions (unverified). Amber is both a possible vendor and a possible "employment agency", so it sits in the liability path.

## 5. Candidate data and platform rules

## 6. Truthfulness and advertising

- Email headers and subject lines must not mislead (https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business, n.d.; https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-bpc/division-7/part-3/chapter-1/article-1-8/section-17529-5/, 2025 text).
- FTC fake-review and endorsement rules, AI-claims enforcement, and California's bot-disclosure law: not researched, unverified. EU AI Act Art.50 disclosure duties apply from 2026-08-02 [S] (https://www.technology.org/2026/10/13/eu-ai-act-deadlines-after-the-digital-omnibus-what-applies-from-december-2026-to-august-2028/, 2026-10-13).
- **Never cross:** pretending a person wrote or read something they did not; a chat or auto-reply persona that hides it is an AI; invented familiarity ("loved your talk", fake mutual connections); invented clients, placements, results or testimonials; claiming a licence or exclusivity Amber lacks; calling a tool "AI ranking" or "bias-free" without proof. A real, named human who has reviewed and sent each message is the right model. **[LAWYER]** to review website claims.

## 7. Business formation at $0 (all unverified: no source was retrieved; confirm with the City of Los Angeles Office of Finance and a California attorney)

- California has no single general business licence; Los Angeles generally requires a Business Tax Registration Certificate, including for home-based businesses. A fictitious name needs a DBA filing with the county. An LLC carries an annual franchise tax (about $800, to confirm) that a sole proprietorship avoids, but a sole proprietorship gives no liability shield.
- Sales tax: consulting and recruiting services are generally not taxed in California; a seller's permit is needed only for tangible goods. Lists or data products sold into other states may be taxable once nexus thresholds are met. **[CPA]**

- **Lead-generation or sourcing vendor versus employment agency:** the vendor is paid by the employer a fee that does not depend on hiring, does not represent or place candidates, and leaves all decisions to the client. The agency is paid for, or on the strength of, a placement. **[LAWYER]** to draft the contract so the substance matches the label.

## 8. Safe operating envelope for the first 90 days (2026-10-19 to about 2027-01-17)

1. **Business model.** Sell sourcing, research and shortlist support to employers and agencies for a fixed or hourly fee agreed in writing first. The fee never depends on a hire and no candidate ever pays. Amber does not employ, represent, negotiate for or "place" anyone; the client decides. No success-fee or split-fee deals. No engagements tied to NY, NJ, MA, IL, Ontario, Quebec, Italy, Spain or Singapore until a lawyer clears them.
2. **Candidates.** Free. They apply directly or opt in. No paid résumé or coaching product.
3. **Outreach.** The owner reads and sends every message; no auto-send. Cold B2B email only to US, UK company, French professional and low-volume Singapore addresses, and only on topics tied to the person's role. Everywhere else: inbound or documented consent only. No cold SMS or calls. Every email has an accurate sender, honest subject, physical address and plain opt-out, honoured within two business days (the law allows ten) and kept on a permanent suppression list, with the source and date of each address recorded. No bought or scraped lists; no tracking pixels in first-touch email to the EU, UK or Canada (unverified rule).
4. **AI.** Drafting, summarising, formatting and de-duplicating only. No scoring, ranking, filtering or rejecting people; no video, voice or emotion analysis. A human reads the source and decides, and the decision is logged. No chat persona; anything AI-facing says it is AI.
5. **Data.** Only what candidates send or what the owner reads by hand on public professional pages. Notice at first contact: who we are, where we got the data, why, how to object or delete. Delete within 30 days of request; default retention 12 months from last contact (design choice, **[LAWYER]**). No sensitive data. No sale or sharing of lists. EU and UK individuals only on inbound requests until representative and transfer questions are settled.
6. **Claims.** No client, placement, result or testimonial unless real, provable and approved by the named party. No invented familiarity. No "licensed", "exclusive" or "AI ranking" claims.
7. **Paperwork.** Los Angeles tax registration and DBA or LLC, chosen with advice; one California attorney review of the client contract and privacy notice.
