Written 19 Oct 2026 (UTC), after the owner's request that Amber's mail be proven to reach inboxes before any volume. **No message has been sent for this work, and no inbox test has been run: the build environment cannot reach DNS, the mail provider or any mailbox, and Amber has no credential for a test inbox.** Everything below is labelled as read from code, built and tested offline, or not verified.

## 1. Why the owner's mail from Amber lands in spam: what the code shows, and what it cannot

Confirmed from the code (`src/lib/integrations/mail.ts`, `src/app/api/valora-relay/mail/route.ts`):
- Mail goes through Resend (or SMTP). The From address is the vault secret `MAIL_FROM`, then `SUPPORT_FROM_EMAIL`, and **if neither is set, `Amber <onboarding@resend.dev>`**. Mail from `resend.dev` is a shared test domain: it cannot be authenticated as Amber's own, which makes spam placement likely (stated from general knowledge, not measured). Whether the production vault has `MAIL_FROM` set, and whether the mail that reached the owner's spam folder came from that fallback, were not checked (no vault access).
- The shared mailer (`sendViaResend` and the SMTP path) accepts **no custom headers at all**, so a `List-Unsubscribe` header cannot be set even by a caller that wants one; `Reply-To` only where a caller passes it. Resend's per-domain open/click tracking (which rewrites links) was not checked: it must be off for cold notes.
- **The shared suppression table `SuppressedRecipient` is in the schema but in no migration** (only six migrations exist; the project builds its schema with `prisma db push`, which would create it). A code comment in the Valora relay route says the table "was never actually created" in production and skips the check there; `sendTransactionalEmail` awaits the same check with no guard. **Whether the table exists in the production database is unverified.** Existing shared endpoints that matter once a sender exists: a GET-only HMAC-signed unsubscribe route that writes to that shared table (not to the lane's decision log), and a Svix-verified Resend webhook that suppresses on bounce and complaint (not connected to the lane's send history). Nothing was changed in shared code.

Not verified (needs access I do not have): the SPF, DKIM and DMARC records of the sending domain, the domain's age and reputation, Resend's domain status and delivery log, the actual headers of a message that landed in spam (the single most useful evidence: Gmail → "Show original" → the Authentication-Results lines). `scripts/recruiting-deliverability-check.ts <domain>` reads the three DNS records and judges them with `checkAuthRecords`; here it reports that DNS cannot be resolved, and says so rather than reporting "no records".

## 2. What was built (offline, tested, not wired to any sender)

`src/lib/recruiting/deliverability.ts` (tests in `deliverability.test.ts`):
- **`checkAuthRecords`**: one SPF record, not open (`+all`/`?all`), with a top-level lookup count (nested includes are not expanded, so the 10-lookup limit is only partly checked); a usable DKIM key; a DMARC record with a policy and a `rua` address; DKIM and return-path alignment (relaxed, by approximate organizational domain) when those domains are supplied. The DNS script supplies the return-path domain only; the DKIM signing domain must be read from the `d=` tag of a real message's header.
- **`checkMessage`**: the habits filters and provider rules punish: shared/free From domain, From domain not the authenticated one, From name not matching the signer, no-reply with no Reply-To, fake `Re:` subjects, link shorteners, tracking pixels, several links, styled HTML, missing postal address or plain way to stop, missing `List-Unsubscribe`, and (for bulk) missing one-click unsubscribe. Every rule pushes toward plain, identified, easy-to-stop mail; none tries to evade a filter.
- **`planSendBatch`**: 20 a day, doubling every three send-days (days with no sending do not count), ceiling 100 until the owner raises it; one note per recipient domain per day (free-mail domains exempt, one per business); one first note and at most one follow-up per business, six days apart; never to a business that is suppressed, replied, complained or unsubscribed, nor to the recipient domain of a complaint or unsubscribe, nor to an address that bounced hard, was blocked or soft-bounced three times (plus-tags and case ignored); addresses are normalized and unusable ones refused. **Automatic pause** on any authentication failure, on 5 hard bounces or blocks, on more than 2% of 20 or more known outcomes, on 2 complaints or more than 0.1%, and when 20 or more sends older than 3 days have no recorded outcome (feedback not arriving). Gmail publishes its own complaint rate only in Postmaster Tools (aim under 0.1%, never 0.3%); those numbers are not in this history, so the owner reads them before raising the ceiling.
- **`classifyBounce`**: by enhanced status first: hard (5.1.x, 5.4.1), soft (4.x, 5.2.2 mailbox full), block (other 5.7.x), auth (5.7.26, 5.7.509, DMARC/SPF/DKIM wording: our own authentication, which pauses sending and suppresses no one); `outcomeOfBounce` maps them to history outcomes.

There is still **no sending code in the lane**. The owner reads and sends each note by hand from their own mailbox. Thousands of messages cannot be sent that way, and a bulk sender would be new code with its own review; it is not built and will not be without the owner's decision. When it is: send from a **dedicated subdomain** used for nothing else (so a complaint cannot hurt the product mail), authenticate it first, run `checkAuthRecords` and `checkMessage` on every message, and let `planSendBatch` decide who may go.

## 3. Human quality of the notes

The composed agency notes and the employer example pass the wording check and the message checks (no errors, no warnings on 40 notes with a plain-text sender and a List-Unsubscribe header). **The independent reviewer's reading of the same notes was less kind** and is the honest quality statement: 18 of 20 open on "my software flagged ..." or "I am writing because ..."; for roles that are not recruiter roles (office coordinator, payroll) the hook does not explain why the sender cares; no note states a benefit beyond "here is data"; some lines are defensive or stiff ("I should say up front that I place no one", "Hello to everyone at ..."); the five-line footer is the same in every note. One real defect was fixed: subjects of the form "Your Sourcer listing at <firm>" read like a reply about the recipient's own job post and were dropped; every subject now states the offer, with no "free". The wording problems are **not** fixed: a person has to judge whether they matter, and the first batch of real notes should be read by the owner before any volume.

**The business model, as the owner described it** (no upfront fee; pay only after a successful placement under an agreed contract). This is **not in any note and the composer does not say it**, for a reason the owner needs to decide on: the product Amber offers today is a $99 pilot / $149 a month research list, and fees tied to a hire are the placement-fee model that 02-voice-and-compliance and 09-viability hold back (employment-agency licensing in California, New York, New Jersey, Massachusetts, Illinois and elsewhere; a lawyer first). Wording, ready for when that decision is made (not in use; as fragments the wording linter flags them only for lacking a named fact, which a full note would supply):
- *Agency (referral partnership):* "I'm not asking for anything up front. If a candidate I introduce is placed with one of your clients, we would agree the referral terms in writing first, and you would pay only if the placement happens."
- *Employer:* "There is no fee to talk or to see candidates. If you hire someone I introduced, the fee is agreed in writing before anything starts and is due only after they begin work."
Sending either without the licence/lawyer answer, or in a note while the offer is the list, would be a claim the business cannot yet back. Note also that Amber has no candidates, so the agency sentence about introducing a candidate would be untrue today, and that the held employer example in `marketing.ts` currently says the opposite ("I take nothing that depends on a hire"): the two must be reconciled by the owner's decision, not by wording.

## 4. The proof that is required before live outreach (the gate)

Nothing is activated until every line is true and the owner has authorized sending:
1. The sending subdomain passes `scripts/recruiting-deliverability-check.ts` (SPF, DMARC with reports, return-path alignment, a DKIM key at the selector read from a real message) run where DNS works, and the DKIM signing domain read from a real message's header is aligned.
2. `MAIL_FROM` is the authenticated subdomain; never `resend.dev`.
3. Seed test: messages sent from that subdomain to authorized test inboxes at Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo and at least one business-hosted mailbox (Google Workspace, Microsoft 365), with the placement (primary / promotions / spam) and the raw Authentication-Results header recorded for each, for both channels (agency note, employer note), on at least three different days. Target: the primary inbox or equivalent in nearly all, no spam at a major provider; any spam result is investigated from its headers, not tuned around.
4. Warm-up with `planSendBatch` limits, with bounce and complaint outcomes fed back; the automatic pause is tested.
5. An independent reviewer reads the content and the results.

**What the owner would need to provide:** the Resend domain settings and `MAIL_FROM` value (read-only), the raw headers of one of the mails that landed in spam, DNS access for the check (or run the script from a laptop), and authorized test mailboxes whose credentials never pass through chat (app passwords stored in the vault); plus network access for the seed test, which is a separate, smaller allow-list request from the 12 domains for the live demonstration. This is the minimum; none of it has been granted.

### Added later (offline)
- `seed-tests.ts` judges the real inbox test from records entered after the seed run: every provider (Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo), a business mailbox, both channels, three different days, nothing in spam or missing, spf, dkim and dmarc each `pass` in the received header, and the DKIM signing domain equal to the sending domain. It sends nothing and reads no mailbox. Until it says "passed", `inboxTestsPassed` stays false.
- `feedback.ts` turns a hard bounce, a block or a complaint from the provider into a do-not-contact entry. It is not connected to the webhook: the shared Resend webhook (`/api/webhooks/resend`) belongs to the support mail and was left alone.

## Independent review of this work

A reviewer who had not written it found no critical problem and six high ones in the first version, all fixed and tested (`deliverability.test.ts`, 22 tests): the plan did not count touches (a third note could be planned); a complaint did not suppress the business or domain; blocks were never suppressed and several bounce kinds were misclassified (including blaming the recipient for our own DMARC failure); the pause could not fire with few outcomes known; the DNS script did not check alignment and failed for a sending subdomain without NS records or with DMARC on the parent. It also found medium problems (idle days inflating the cap, time zones, address normalization, DKIM and SPF false errors, weak opt-out and postal-address patterns), all fixed. Its remaining observations stand as written above: the mailer cannot set headers, no one-click unsubscribe endpoint writes to the lane's log, bounce and complaint feedback has no path into the lane, and the notes' wording tells.
