BIO 101 - Cellular respiration (lecture 7 notes)

Big picture: cells break down glucose to make ATP. Overall equation: C6H12O6 + 6 O2 -> 6 CO2 + 6 H2O + energy. Eukaryotes get roughly 30-32 ATP per glucose (older textbooks say 36-38).

1. Glycolysis
- Happens in the cytoplasm. Does NOT need oxygen.
- Glucose (6 carbons) is split into 2 pyruvate (3 carbons each).
- Uses 2 ATP early on and makes 4 ATP, so the net gain is 2 ATP, plus 2 NADH.

2. Pyruvate oxidation
- Pyruvate enters the mitochondrial matrix.
- Each pyruvate becomes acetyl-CoA, releasing 1 CO2 and making 1 NADH.

3. Krebs cycle (citric acid cycle)
- In the mitochondrial matrix. Acetyl-CoA joins oxaloacetate to make citrate.
- Per glucose (two turns of the cycle): 2 ATP, 6 NADH, 2 FADH2 and 4 CO2.

4. Electron transport chain + chemiosmosis
- On the inner mitochondrial membrane. NADH and FADH2 hand their electrons to protein complexes.
- Energy from the electrons pumps H+ across the membrane, building a proton gradient.
- H+ flows back through ATP synthase, which makes most of the ATP (about 26-28 per glucose).
- Oxygen is the final electron acceptor and ends up as water. With no oxygen the chain backs up.

5. No oxygen? Fermentation
- Glycolysis can keep going if NAD+ is regenerated.
- Lactic acid fermentation (muscle cells): pyruvate -> lactate.
- Alcohol fermentation (yeast): pyruvate -> ethanol + CO2.
- Only the 2 ATP from glycolysis per glucose.

Exam traps
- Most ATP comes from the electron transport chain, not from glycolysis or the Krebs cycle.
- CO2 is released in pyruvate oxidation and in the Krebs cycle: 6 CO2 per glucose in total.
- The water in the overall equation is made at the end of the electron transport chain, not in glycolysis.
