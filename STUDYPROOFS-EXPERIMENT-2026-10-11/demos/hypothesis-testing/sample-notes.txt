STAT 200 - Hypothesis testing basics (review sheet)

Setup
- Null hypothesis H0: the "nothing is happening" claim (for example, the mean score is 70).
- Alternative hypothesis H1: what we suspect instead (the mean is not 70, or is greater than 70).
- Test statistic: how far the sample result is from H0, measured in standard errors.
- Significance level alpha: the cutoff we choose before looking at the data, commonly 0.05.

The p-value
- The p-value is the probability of getting a result at least as extreme as ours IF H0 is true.
- Decision rule: if p <= alpha, reject H0. If p > alpha, fail to reject H0.
- A small p-value is NOT the probability that H0 is true.
- "Fail to reject" does not prove H0. It means we did not find enough evidence against it.

Errors
- Type I error: rejecting H0 when H0 is actually true. Its probability is alpha.
- Type II error: failing to reject H0 when H0 is actually false. Its probability is beta.
- Power = 1 - beta, the chance of correctly rejecting a false H0. Larger samples and larger true effects raise power.

Worked example (z-test)
- Claim: the average exam score is 70. The population standard deviation is sigma = 12 (known).
- Sample: n = 36 students, sample mean = 74.
- z = (74 - 70) / (12 / sqrt(36)) = 4 / 2 = 2.0
- The two-sided p-value for z = 2.0 is about 0.0455.
- Since 0.0455 < 0.05, we reject H0 at the 5% level: the data are evidence that the true mean is not 70.

Common mistakes
- Choosing alpha after seeing the p-value.
- Saying "we are 95% sure H0 is false".
- Confusing statistical significance with practical importance.
