## 4. What is measured, and what decides

- **The tracker** (amberai #105, marker `amber-studyproofs-tracker`) already counts, per source: visitors, signups, first study set (the student made material), came back (the student used it again on a later day), free trials, checkouts, verified live payments. Sources in this test: `youtube`, `linkedin`, `x`, `facebook`, `examples` (the pages' own try-it links), and `discord` if used. It also lists referring domains, which catches a YouTube viewer who types the address rather than clicking.
- **The ledger:** one line per post in `LAUNCH-LEDGER.md` (created on day 1): date, platform, post URL, `src`, `campaign`. Amber fills in each source's numbers on day 7.
- **The reading on day 7, a rough decision rule.** The numbers from eight posts in one week are small, so this is a rule for what to try next, not proof of demand and not proof that a channel cannot reach students. As a working rule: a source with 5 or more signups where at least half made a first study set is worth that channel's part of the larger plan next. Visitors but no signups points at the page or the offer rather than the channel. Signups but no first set points at the first upload step, the next thing to fix before more promotion. No visit from any source after eight posts says only that these particular posts on these accounts did not bring anyone in that week; it does not settle whether the accounts can reach students, and whether to try the larger plan's new-account channels (TikTok, Instagram, Pinterest) stays your call.
- **Cost:** $0. Nothing is boosted, no listing is bought, no tool is subscribed to.

## 5. The support flow, as deployed (#821 and #822, live since 17:33Z on 2026-10-13), and the address found on 2026-10-13 at 21:3xZ

**The design.** The support address StudyProofs shows comes from one setting read at boot (the vault entry `STUDYPROOFS_SUPPORT_EMAIL`, else the business profile's public contact address, else the code's default); the owner's notification addresses are never the public contact. That address keeps its own mailbox; its mail provider forwards a copy of each message to Amber's existing Resend inbox (`support@` on the receiving domain Resend already serves, derived at boot). The signed webhook keeps each forwarded message as one ticket, Amber categorises it, automatic replies to customers stay off, and what needs you reaches your notification address through the app's verified sender. Amber's end-to-end test sends a marked message from that sender to the public address and watches it come back as a ticket and get handed over; when it first passes you get one email, and the tracker's verdict flips when the production walk passes.

**What production showed (support-mail readings of 19:15Z and 20:39Z, probe walk of 20:39Z).** Both services run the design, replies are off, the Resend inbox target is listed, addresses are shown masked. The business profile's contact address is on a domain with no mail routing, so it cannot be the support address. The probe also found that the pages' footer still showed the code's default address: the landing, signup, login and legal pages were prerendered at build, where the chosen address does not exist. Draft #824 makes them read it when each request arrives.

**The address found, and its provider.** A read-only lookup of the brand domains' public DNS (a GitHub Actions workflow with no secrets, addresses masked in its output) found three domains that receive mail: mokiplay.com (Forward Email, free plan, the forwarding in the domain's own TXT record), myreelo.com (Cloudflare Email Routing, rules not visible from outside) and launchready.ai (Google Workspace, addresses not visible from outside); amberoneai.com, launchreadyal.com, meetdayli.com, restpilotai.com, solvestackai.com and studyproofs.com receive none. The only existing address whose existence, routing and provider could all be verified is the `support` alias on mokiplay.com, which the repository's own pages publish and whose TXT record forwards it (and `hello`) to one personal Gmail address, the original mailbox. It is the chosen support address: `s***@mokiplay.com`. The trade-off is that it carries another brand's domain; it is an existing business support inbox, which is what was authorised.

**The forwarding and confirmation steps (owner-only).** (1) In Cloudflare DNS for mokiplay.com, edit the apex TXT record that starts with `forward-email=` so the `support` alias lists a second recipient, Amber's Resend inbox address `support@inbound.myreelo.com`, next to the existing one (the alias repeated with a comma, as Forward Email's documentation describes; the line stays under 255 characters); every recipient gets a copy and your Gmail keeps receiving the original. No confirmation code is involved with Forward Email. (2) In HQ's Credential Vault add `STUDYPROOFS_SUPPORT_EMAIL` with the alias's full address; both services read it at their next restart (any deploy). Amber then sees the forwarding in the public domain's own record (the `forwarding-record` step of the setup pass, from #823) and runs her end-to-end test; the first pass emails you "Support address works" and shows in the inbox comment on #105.

**The inbound test (the gate for this launch test).** First the Resend inbox path on its own, possible today: a message from any personal account to `support@inbound.myreelo.com` must become a ticket and reach your notification address. Then the whole chain after the two steps: a message to the chosen address must arrive in your Gmail, become a ticket through the forwarded copy, and reach your notification address. StudyProofs is not called launch-ready, and this test stays unpublished, until that live email-to-ticket-to-owner-notification test passes and you approve the exact posts.
